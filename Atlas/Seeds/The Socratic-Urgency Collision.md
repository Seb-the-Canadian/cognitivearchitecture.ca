# SEED-29: The Socratic-Urgency Collision

**Theme:** Resolving slowness vs. speed tension; scoped questioning within urgent timelines

**Bridge:** Socratic prototyping ↔ Governance urgency (CLAIM-governance-enables-ai-scale) ↔ Three-phase implementation

---

## Working Title

When "Start with Questions" Meets "Build Now or Lose"

## One-Sentence Thesis

Socratic prototyping's deliberate slowness collides with governance urgency's "build the moat now"—and resolving this tension reveals when pausing is strategic and when it's avoidance.

## Reader Promise

Understand how to apply question-first methodology in contexts where competitive pressure demands speed, without sacrificing either.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Socratic Prototyping]] ↔ [[Governance Enables AI Scale]]** — Socratic method says "start with questions"; governance urgency says "build foundation now before window closes." Both are right. The resolution: Socratic questioning applies to *how* you build, not *whether* you build. You can question your approach while maintaining urgency about timing. *Why it matters: resolves apparent contradiction between two valid commitments.*

2. **[[Socratic Prototyping]] ↔ [[Framework - Three-Phase Governance Implementation]]** — Socratic method embeds questioning into each phase; phased implementation provides the container for questions. Phase 1 is the Socratic period (establish culture through questioning); Phase 2 applies what Phase 1 learned. The three-phase model is Socratic prototyping at organizational scale. *Why it matters: operationalizes Socratic approach within governance timeline.*

## Core Throughline

- Slowness and urgency aren't opposites when scoped correctly
- Socratic questioning applies to approach, not timeline
- Three-phase model embeds questions in Phase 1 while maintaining urgency for Phase 2
