# SEED-23: Translation Over Transmission

**Theme:** AI enablement philosophy; why teaching meaning beats teaching features

**Bridge:** Philosophy of enablement ↔ Semantic clarity ↔ Data fluency development

---

## Working Title

Why AI Training Fails When It Teaches Features Instead of Meaning

## One-Sentence Thesis

Most AI enablement transmits features ("here's how to write a prompt") rather than translating tools into human terms ("here's why this matters for your work")—and this explains why training completion doesn't predict adoption.

## Reader Promise

Understand the difference between transmission and translation in AI enablement, and why translation produces lasting capability.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Philosophy of Enablement]] ↔ [[Semantic Clarity Prerequisite]]** — "Translation over transmission" is semantic clarity applied to enablement: ensuring shared meaning before shared tools. Most AI training assumes semantic clarity exists (everyone knows what "prompt" means) when it doesn't. Translation builds the clarity that enables adoption. *Why it matters: connects enablement philosophy to knowledge systems framework.*

2. **[[Philosophy of Enablement]] ↔ [[Framework - Data Fluency Development Progression]]** — The enablement philosophy's "scaffolding over systems" parallels fluency development's stages: both recognize that capability develops through use in context, not through one-time training. Translation creates scaffolds; transmission creates compliance. *Why it matters: bridges organizational enablement to individual capability development.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Transmission teaches features; translation teaches meaning
- Training completion ≠ adoption because meaning is missing
- Scaffolding that grows with people enables lasting capability
