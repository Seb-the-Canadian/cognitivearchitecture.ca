# The Seven Stewardships

**Theme:** Stewardship role decomposition; sustainable knowledge work practice

**Bridge:** Conservatory Roles ↔ Member Data Stewardship ↔ SEED-25 (Stewardship Burnout)

---

## Working Title

Stewardship Is Not One Job—It Is Seven

## One-Sentence Thesis

Organizations that treat "data steward" as a single role create generalist burnout; decomposing stewardship into seven distinct practices—cultivation, mapping, optimization, preservation, cross-linking, synthesis, and boundary management—enables sustainable, specialized care.

## Reader Promise

Learn why your stewards are overwhelmed and how role specialization prevents burnout while deepening expertise.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Conservatory Roles]] ↔ [[Member Data Stewardship]]** — The seven Conservatory Roles (Gardener, Cartographer, Refactorer, Archivist, Pollinator, Voice, Bridgekeeper) operationalize "stewardship" as distinct practices rather than a single identity. Each role requires different skills, different cognitive modes, different energy. *Why it matters: provides concrete decomposition for stewardship culture programs.*

2. **[[Conservatory Roles]] ↔ [[The Stewardship Burnout Paradox]]— SEED-25 diagnoses burnout as "individuals absorbing organizational gaps." Role decomposition addresses this directly: instead of one person doing everything, specialized roles share the load. Burnout = trying to be all seven at once. *Why it matters: provides structural solution to burnout problem.*

3. **[[Conservatory Roles]] ↔ [[Framework - Three Enabling Mechanisms]]** — The seven roles instantiate all three mechanisms: shared vocabulary (Language), role-specific practices (Literacy), and rotation/feedback (Loops). Roles ARE the mechanisms at personal scale. *Why it matters: demonstrates mechanisms can be embedded in practice design.*

## Core Throughline

- Stewardship fails when treated as single generalist role
- Seven distinct practices require different skills and energy
- Role rotation prevents fatigue; role depth enables expertise
- This isn't cosplay—it's structured cognitive allocation

## First Paragraph Starter

> "Be a good steward." The phrase sounds simple. But what does it actually ask? Tend growing things (Gardener). Map connections (Cartographer). Preserve what matters (Archivist). Simplify complexity (Refactorer). Cross-pollinate ideas (Pollinator). Synthesize for others (Voice). Guard boundaries (Bridgekeeper). Seven distinct cognitive practices, seven different energy types—and we ask one person to do them all, simultaneously, indefinitely. No wonder your stewards are exhausted.
