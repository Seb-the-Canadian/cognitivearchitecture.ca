# Transmission as Completion

**Theme:** Capability development; organizational sustainability; stewardship scaling

**Bridge:** Framework - Skill Tree Development ↔ SEED-25 (Stewardship Burnout) ↔ Data Fluency Development

---

## Working Title

A Skill Isn't Complete Until You Can Teach It

## One-Sentence Thesis

Skill Tree's Tier 3 (Transmissible Craft) marks the difference between personal mastery and organizational capability—when stewardship remains at Tier 2 (mastery without transmission), organizations depend on individuals who burn out; transmission is not bonus, it's completion.

## Reader Promise

Understand why your organization keeps losing capability when key people leave, and how transmission transforms individual skill into organizational infrastructure.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Framework - Skill Tree Development]] ↔ [[Framework - Data Fluency Development Progression]]** — Data Fluency stages describe personal development (literacy → fluency → mastery). Skill Tree adds what's missing: Tier 3 (Transmissible Craft). Personal mastery isn't the end—organizational transmission is. Together they form complete capability model. *Why it matters: extends existing framework with transmission requirement.*

2. **[[Framework - Skill Tree Development]] ↔ [[SEED-25-The-Stewardship-Burnout-Paradox]]** — SEED-25 identifies burnout as "individuals absorbing organizational gaps." Tier 3 directly addresses this: transmission distributes capability so individuals don't have to absorb everything. Burnout = capability stuck at Tier 2. *Why it matters: provides structural solution to burnout problem.*

3. **[[Framework - Skill Tree Development]] ↔ [[Philosophy of Enablement]]** — Philosophy of Enablement argues "translation over transmission" for AI enablement. But for human capability, transmission IS the goal—capability that transfers. Enablement creates the scaffolding; transmission creates the persistence. *Why it matters: distinguishes capability transmission from content transmission.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Personal mastery isn't the end
- Organizational capability requires transmission
- Tier 2 mastery without Tier 3 transmission = burnout risk
- Transmission is completion, not bonus

## The Three Tiers

| Tier | Focus | Completion Signal |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| **Tier 1: Basic Aptitude** | Personal capability acquisition | Can perform independently |
| **Tier 2: Mastery Pattern** | Contextual expertise | Can handle complexity without guidance |
| **Tier 3: Transmissible Craft** | Organizational capability building | Others can perform because of your teaching |

---

## Burnout Diagnosis

| Symptom | Tier Diagnosis | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Individual absorbs all gaps | Capability stuck at Tier 2 | Invest in Tier 3 development |
| Knowledge loss on departure | Tier 3 never reached | Build transmission infrastructure |
| Single points of failure | No redundancy in capability | Distribute through teaching |
| "Only X knows how to do this" | Mastery without transmission | Create guides, onboard successors |

## Tension / Counterpoint

- **Counterpoint:** Not everyone needs to teach; some skills are specialist and shouldn't be democratized
- **Your position:** Fair point for rare expertise. But most organizational capability isn't specialist—it's crucial. If "only X knows how" describes core functions, that's fragility, not specialization. Transmission isn't about democratizing everything; it's about not creating single points of failure for what matters.
