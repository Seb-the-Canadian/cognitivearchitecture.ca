# SEED-17: Consent Interfaces as Trust Architecture

**Theme:** Consent design as competitive differentiator; trust made tangible in UX

**Bridge:** Only-once principle ↔ Architecture shapes behavior ↔ Digital sovereignty ↔ Member data stewardship

---

## Working Title

When Consent Design Becomes Competitive Advantage

## One-Sentence Thesis

In open banking, consent interface quality becomes directly measurable trust—and organizations that design consent for member experience rather than legal compliance will win members from those that don't.

## Reader Promise

Understand how consent design differentiates trust-based organizations from extraction-based ones, and what member-centric consent actually looks like.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Concept - Only-Once Principle]] ↔ [[Default Decisions - Architecture Shapes Behaviour]]** — Only-once principle is consent architecture: it shapes what's easy (single consent) vs. hard (repeated consent). But most consent architecture does the opposite: makes consent hard (buried settings, repeated asks) because friction serves extraction. Estonia's model inverts the architecture: consent defaults to member control. *Why it matters: connects specific principle (only-once) to broader architectural analysis.*

2. **[[Digital Sovereignty]] ↔ [[Member Data Stewardship]]** — Sovereignty is abstract until operationalized through stewardship. Consent interfaces are where sovereignty becomes tangible: can the member actually control their data, or just acknowledge they've been told about it? The gap between "informed" consent and "genuine" consent is the gap between sovereignty rhetoric and sovereignty reality. *Why it matters: grounds abstract sovereignty concept in observable design choices.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Consent design reveals whether organization values member control or legal coverage
- In open banking, members can choose based on consent experience (switching easy)
- Organizations that design consent for trust will outcompete those that design for compliance

## First Paragraph Starter

> Read your consent interface. Not the policy—the actual screen members see. Is it designed for them to understand, or for you to prove you told them? The difference is trust architecture. In open banking, members can switch institutions easily. They'll choose the one where consent feels like respect, not extraction. Consent design becomes competitive advantage—not because regulators require it, but because members experience it.
