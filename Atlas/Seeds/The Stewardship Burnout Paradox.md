# SEED-25: The Stewardship Burnout Paradox

**Theme:** Care labor in governance culture; unsustainable stewardship design

**Bridge:** Member data stewardship ↔ Philosophy of enablement ↔ Three enabling mechanisms

---

## Working Title

When Care Becomes Crushing: The Hidden Cost of Stewardship Culture

## One-Sentence Thesis

Organizations that successfully build stewardship culture often burn out the very people who embody it—because systems designed for compliance don't support the emotional labor of genuine care.

## Reader Promise

Understand why your best stewards are exhausted, and what organizational design would protect them.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Member Data Stewardship]] ↔ [[Philosophy of Enablement]]** — Stewardship asks people to care; enablement philosophy recognizes care requires scaffolding. Stewardship without enablement infrastructure creates "care debt"—individuals absorbing organizational gaps. *Why it matters: links stewardship success to enablement failure mode.*

2. **[[Philosophy of Enablement]] ↔ [[Framework - Three Enabling Mechanisms]]** — "Scaffolding over systems" (enablement) parallels "feedback loops" (governance mechanisms). Both recognize that sustainable capability requires organizational support, not just individual commitment. Stewardship culture needs the same feedback infrastructure that governance culture needs. *Why it matters: applies personal enablement principles to organizational governance design.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Stewardship succeeds when individuals absorb gaps; this is unsustainable
- Enablement philosophy diagnoses the problem: scaffolding missing
- Three mechanisms framework provides the scaffold: language + literacy + feedback

> Your best data stewards are exhausted.
