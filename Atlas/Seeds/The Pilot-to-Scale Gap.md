# SEED-18: The Pilot-to-Scale Gap

**Theme:** Why transformations die between pilot and scale; protected conditions that don't transfer

**Bridge:** Three-phase governance implementation ↔ Semantic clarity prerequisite ↔ Data fluency ↔ Hesitation-to-learning loop

---

## Working Title

Why 30% of Transformations Die Between Pilot and Scale

## One-Sentence Thesis

Pilots succeed because they have protected conditions (attention, resources, enthusiasts); scaling fails because those conditions don't transfer—and the gap is cultural, not technical.

## Reader Promise

Understand the specific mechanisms that kill transformations after successful pilots, and what conditions enable scaling.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Framework - Three-Phase Governance Implementation]] ↔ [[Semantic Clarity Prerequisite]]** — Pilot success often masks semantic clarity absence. Small, enthusiastic teams create shared understanding informally; scaling exposes the lack of explicit clarity. The pilot-to-scale gap is often a clarity gap that was papered over by team proximity. *Why it matters: explains why pilots succeed and scale fails—it's not the tool, it's the hidden prerequisite.*

2. **[[Data Fluency]] ↔ [[Framework - Hesitation-to-Learning Loop]]** — Pilots often work because early adopters are already fluent (or become fluent through intensive engagement). Scaling requires fluency across broader population—including people who haven't had intensive engagement. The learning loop that made pilots work needs to scale, not just the tool. *Why it matters: connects individual capability development to organizational scaling dynamics.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Pilots succeed with protected conditions that don't transfer
- The gap is cultural (clarity, fluency, safety) not technical
- Scaling requires making implicit success conditions explicit

## First Paragraph Starter

> The pilot worked beautifully. High adoption, enthusiastic users, measurable impact. Then you scaled. Adoption dropped. Complaints rose. The same tool that succeeded in pilot failed at scale. The tool didn't change—the conditions did. Pilots have protected conditions: leadership attention, dedicated resources, enthusiastic early adopters, small teams with informal shared understanding. Scaling exposes every assumption the pilot didn't have to test.
