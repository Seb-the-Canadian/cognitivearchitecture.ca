# SEED-31: The Enablement-Extraction Spectrum

**Theme:** AI program design; distinguishing capability-building from dependency-creation

**Bridge:** [[Philosophy of Enablement]] ↔ [[capital cognitive capture]] ↔ [[Digital Sovereignty]]

---

## Working Title

Is Your AI Enablement Actually AI Extraction?

## One-Sentence Thesis

AI enablement sits on a spectrum with extraction—the difference is whether capability stays with the person or accrues to the platform, and most "enablement" programs are actually extraction wearing enablement language.

## Reader Promise

Diagnose whether your AI enablement builds human capability or platform dependency, and what the difference requires.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Philosophy of Enablement]] ↔ [[Capital Cognitive Capture]]** — Enablement empowers with care; extraction captures without consent. AI "enablement" often captures: the capability accrues to the platform (workflow data, prompt patterns, interaction models) while the human becomes dependent. True enablement builds capability the person keeps if the platform disappears. *Why it matters: provides diagnostic for distinguishing enablement from extraction.*

2. **[[Philosophy of Enablement]] ↔ [[Digital Sovereignty]]** — Enablement philosophy's "digital sovereignty" principle operationalized: does AI training build platform-independent capability? Can the person prompt effectively in any AI system, or only yours? Sovereignty-preserving enablement is rarer than it sounds—most training creates platform lock-in. *Why it matters: positions sovereignty as enablement design criterion.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Most AI enablement creates dependency, not capability
- Extraction wears enablement language ("upskilling" that locks in)
- True enablement builds transferable, platform-independent skills

## First Paragraph Starter

> You're running an AI enablement program. Completion rates are high. Satisfaction scores are strong. But answer this: if you switched AI platforms tomorrow, would your people's capability transfer? Or did you just train them to depend on one system? Most AI "enablement" builds platform dependency, not human capability. The workflow optimizations accrue to the platform; the prompt patterns work only in one context. That's not enablement—it's extraction wearing enablement language.
