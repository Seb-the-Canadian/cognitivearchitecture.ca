# SEED-20: Knowledge Stewardship as Civic Practice

**Theme:** Personal to collective scale; digital gardening as civic infrastructure foundation

**Bridge:** Digital gardening ↔ Social infrastructure ↔ Cognitive conservatory ↔ Member data stewardship

---

## Working Title

From Personal Knowledge Management to Collective Infrastructure

## One-Sentence Thesis

Digital gardening starts as personal practice but becomes civic responsibility when we recognize that collective knowledge infrastructure requires stewards who treat knowledge as commons rather than commodity.

## Reader Promise

See how personal knowledge practices scale to collective infrastructure, and what civic knowledge stewardship demands.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[digital garden]] ↔ [[Social Infrastructure]]** — Digital gardening is personal social infrastructure: tending knowledge the way parks departments tend public space. The conservatory vision scales this: just as civic infrastructure requires maintenance funding and stewardship norms, cognitive infrastructure requires knowledge stewards who treat their work as civic contribution. *Why it matters: connects individual practice to collective vision.*

2. **[[The Cognitive Conservatory]] ↔ [[Member Data Stewardship]]** — Organizational stewardship (member data) and civic stewardship (cognitive conservatory) share the same orientation: knowledge as commons requiring care. The skills developed in organizational contexts (consent architecture, transparent processing, member-centered design) are precisely the skills civic knowledge infrastructure needs. Organizations become training grounds for civic stewards. *Why it matters: reveals pathway from organizational to civic scale.*

3. **Civic stewardship ↔ [[Horizon Zero Dawn as a Modern Parable — Technological Hubris and the Ethics of Stewardship|Horizon Zero Dawn as a Modern Parable]]** — The stakes of this scaling challenge — from personal practice to civic responsibility — are illustrated in the Horizon parable, where the failure of stewardship at civilizational scale leads to collapse precisely because no one treated knowledge custodianship as civic obligation. *Why it matters: dramatizes what happens when the personal → civic scaling pathway fails.*

## Core Throughline

- Personal knowledge practices develop stewardship skills
- Organizational contexts (especially cooperatives) scale stewardship norms
- Civic infrastructure requires these skills and norms at population scale

## First Paragraph Starter

> You tend your digital garden. Notes connected, ideas developing, knowledge growing. It's satisfying work—slow, authentic, yours. But what you're developing isn't just a personal practice. You're building skills in knowledge stewardship: how to structure for discovery, how to preserve while evolving, how to make knowledge accessible without extracting it. These are civic skills. And we need them at scale, because collective knowledge infrastructure requires stewards who treat knowledge as commons.
