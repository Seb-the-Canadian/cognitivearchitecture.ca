# The Agent-Role Mirror

**Theme:** Human-AI collaboration architecture; cognitive alignment in AI partnership

**Bridge:** AI Agent Mapping ↔ [[Cognitive Architecture]] ↔ [[Conservatory Roles]]

---

## Working Title

Your AI Agents Should Mirror Your Human Roles

## One-Sentence Thesis

When [[Atlas/Notes/Ideas/AI]] assistants are designed without alignment to human epistemic roles, they create cognitive friction; when agents mirror human role functions, human-AI partnership becomes coherent—the mapping is not decoration, it is architecture.

## Reader Promise

Understand why your AI tools feel disjointed and how role-aligned agent design creates partnership instead of friction.

---

## Novel Connections

1. **[[Conservatory Roles]] ↔ [[Cognitive Architecture]]** — Cognitive Architecture calls for "intentional systems design for human-AI partnership." The Agent-Role Mirror is that intention made concrete: each human role (Gardener, Cartographer, etc.) has a corresponding AI agent (Mossling, Glyphwalker, etc.) that supports that specific cognitive mode. *Why it matters: operationalizes abstract architecture principle.*

2. **[[Framework - Conservatory Roles]] ↔ [[Member Data Stewardship]]** — The seven AI agents (Mossling, Glyphwalker, Splinter, Scriptrix, Thistlebee, Echofern, Thresher) map to DAMA governance functions indirectly. This isn't coincidence—stewardship roles have parallel organizational functions. AI agent design should reflect governance alignment. *Why it matters: connects personal AI tools to organizational governance.*

3. **[[Cognitive Architecture]] ↔ [[Tool Congruence]]** — Tool congruence asks whether systems work "with the grain of how you think." Agent-role mirroring is tool congruence applied to AI: does this agent support the cognitive mode I'm in, or fight it? Misaligned agents create the same friction as misaligned tools. *Why it matters: applies tool congruence principle to AI design.*

---

## Core Throughline

- Generic AI assistants create cognitive friction
- Human epistemic work has distinct modes (the seven roles)
- AI agents aligned to these modes reduce friction
- The mapping is architecture, not metaphor

## Tension / Counterpoint

- **Counterpoint:** General-purpose AI is more flexible; role-specific agents create unnecessary complexity
- **Your position:** Flexibility without alignment is friction. You don't want an AI that can do anything—you want one that supports what you're doing *now*. Context-switching between roles is hard enough without your tools fighting you.
